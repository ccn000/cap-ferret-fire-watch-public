Model assumptions

Base-rate prior is built from a structured library of comparable affluent/tourist wildland-interface evacuations, weighted by similarity and date quality, then conditioned on the peninsula not yet being practically reopened.

Likelihood adjustments reward containment, lifted evacuation/access/commercial signals and favourable weather; they penalise active/non-fixed fire, new evacuation extensions, closed roads, absent commerce, adverse weather and unresolved urgent evidence.

This is a decision-support forecast, not an official reopening estimate. The historical case library is small and deliberately hidden from the public UI to avoid over-claiming comparability. The forecast should move sharply if authorities announce the fire is fixed, evacuation orders are lifted, RD106/access routes reopen or businesses confirm normal public service.

Current assessment

What we think now and why

Latest new information · 26 Jul 2026, 13:00

Still worsening regionallyOpen assessmentClose assessment

The latest reviewed official signal is the Gironde prefecture’s 13:00 situation point. It says the Saumos fire continued to progress after a difficult night when the fire became virulent and unpredictable, then eased late in the night as less unfavourable weather allowed responders to resume offensive action. The official toll is now 42,000 hectares burned, nearly 220,000 people preventively evacuated in 23 communes, 75 firefighters treated, 240 homes destroyed, 2,500 firefighters, 18 aircraft, 1,500 military personnel, 1,200 police/gendarmes and renewed A400M engagement. For Cap Ferret, the practical conclusion is unchanged: the peninsula remains evacuated, inbound public access is unavailable, road reopening and essential commerce have not been confirmed, and there is no tourist reopening signal.

What matters now
  • Newest material update: the Gironde prefecture reports 42,000 hectares burned and about 220,000 preventive evacuations in 23 communes.
  • The fire progressed after a virulent, unpredictable night, but less unfavourable weather is letting firefighters resume offensive action.
  • The response footprint is now official at 2,500 firefighters, 18 aircraft, 1,500 military personnel, 1,200 police/gendarmes and the A400M re-engaged.
  • The provisional human and property toll has worsened: 75 firefighters treated and 240 homes destroyed, with no civilian or responder deaths reported at this stage.
  • A63 and departmental road closures reinforce that road access and transit remain disrupted beyond the evacuated peninsula.
  • The Cap Ferret decision remains negative: the peninsula is evacuated, public inbound access is unavailable and no normal tourist reopening has been announced.
Assessment confidence

High confidence that the Cap Ferret practical reopening signal remains negative: the evacuation and no-return instructions have not been lifted, and the newest direct prefecture point reports larger burned area, more evacuations, more damage and continued major response operations. High confidence in the new 42,000-hectare / about 220,000-evacuee / 240-home figures because they come from the Gironde prefecture. High confidence that Bordeaux Airport flight operation remains separate from ground-access risk because the official board continues to list arrivals and departures while publishing a separate hotel-zone evacuation and tram-stop disruption notice.
