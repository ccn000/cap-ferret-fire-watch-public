Current assessment

What we think now and why

Latest new information · 26 Jul 2026, 14:52

Still worsening regionallyOpen assessmentClose assessment

The latest reviewed local signal is a 14:52 CEST TVCapFerret live-thread update relaying the Ville de Lège-Cap-Ferret point: some flare-ups continue around Lège Bourg and Claouey under constant surveillance, the Claouey Sud firebreak 85 widening is almost finished, and the rest of the peninsula shows no fire evolution for now. The broader official baseline remains the Gironde prefecture’s 13:00 situation point: 42,000 hectares burned, nearly 220,000 people preventively evacuated in 23 communes, 75 firefighters treated, 240 homes destroyed, 2,500 firefighters, 18 aircraft, 1,500 military personnel, 1,200 police/gendarmes and renewed A400M engagement. For Cap Ferret, the practical conclusion is unchanged: the peninsula remains evacuated, inbound public access is unavailable, road reopening and essential commerce have not been confirmed, and there is no tourist reopening signal.

What matters now
  • Newest material update: Lège-Cap-Ferret reports flare-ups around Lège Bourg/Claouey, the Claouey Sud firebreak 85 nearly finished, and no fire evolution on the rest of the peninsula for now.
  • The broader official baseline remains severe: the Gironde prefecture reports 42,000 hectares burned and about 220,000 preventive evacuations in 23 communes.
  • The response footprint is official at 2,500 firefighters, 18 aircraft, 1,500 military personnel, 1,200 police/gendarmes and the A400M re-engaged.
  • The provisional human and property toll remains 75 firefighters treated and 240 homes destroyed, with no civilian or responder deaths reported at this stage.
  • A63 and departmental road closures reinforce that road access and transit remain disrupted beyond the evacuated peninsula.
  • The Cap Ferret decision remains negative: the peninsula is evacuated, public inbound access is unavailable and no normal tourist reopening has been announced.
Assessment confidence

High confidence that the Cap Ferret practical reopening signal remains negative: the evacuation and no-return instructions have not been lifted, Inforoutes continues to warn against travel to Saumos, Le Porge and Lège-Cap-Ferret, and the newest local Lège-Cap-Ferret signal still describes flare-ups and defensive firebreak work rather than reopening. High confidence in the 42,000-hectare / about 220,000-evacuee / 240-home figures because they come from the Gironde prefecture. High confidence that Bordeaux Airport flight operation remains separate from ground-access risk because the official board continues to list arrivals and departures while publishing a separate hotel-zone evacuation and tram-stop disruption notice.
