Situation report · Gironde

FULL EVACUATIONVery active and unpredictable fire

The peninsula remains evacuated; the fire is still progressing and not controlled.

The operational picture remains critical and has broadened beyond the peninsula: the midday briefing confirmed more than 10,000 hectares affected, 53 homes and a campsite destroyed, 27 firefighters treated and about 1,000 firefighters deployed. Arès is now under mandatory evacuation and navigation on Arcachon Bay is temporarily prohibited. There is no signal that travelling to Cap Ferret is viable.

Definitive current situation

Do not travel to Cap Ferret: the whole peninsula remains evacuated and the fire is still progressing.

Latest new information · 24 Jul 2026, 13:56

Deteriorating

The decisive facts are the complete evacuation of the Cap Ferret peninsula, a fire still described as uncontrolled and advancing, and the midday expansion of the emergency response. About 1,000 firefighters are now engaged. Authorities report 53 homes and a campsite destroyed, 27 firefighters treated, six of them taken to hospital, and no deaths. Arès has been placed under mandatory evacuation and the maritime prefecture has temporarily prohibited navigation on Arcachon Bay. Inbound road access remains closed to the public.

What matters now
  • The entire peninsula remains evacuated, and mandatory evacuation has extended to Arès on the opposite side of the northern bay.
  • The fire remains uncontrolled; 27 firefighters have required treatment and forecast westerly gusts could reach 80 km/h.
  • Road entry and ordinary navigation on Arcachon Bay are unavailable, so there is no viable land or public-water route for a holiday trip.
  • The next meaningful threshold is official control of the fire and permission to enter, followed by road, ferry, utility, beach, business and forest-access reopening.
Assessment confidence

High confidence on the evacuation and navigation restrictions, the approximately 1,000-firefighter deployment and the absence of a viable travel signal. The midday prefectural briefing gives the clearest damage count: 53 homes and one campsite destroyed, with 2,000 homes saved. Earlier wording that 80 homes were burned or affected should not be read as 80 confirmed total destructions.
